01 · Overview

The model in one minute

Intelligence is information collected, evaluated, integrated, and interpreted to reduce uncertainty and support a decision.
INPUT

Raw reporting

Records what a source, sensor, document, or collector observed or claimed.

JUDGMENT

Analytic assessment

Explains what the available information probably means and why.

DELIVERY

Briefing

Presents selected information or intelligence to a specific audience.

Essential distinction: intelligence is the substance, a report is a durable product, and a briefing is a tailored delivery method.
02 · Foundations

What intelligence means

The term has three related meanings:

Product

Knowledge, reporting, analysis, warning, assessment, or estimate.

Process

Direction, collection, processing, analysis, production, dissemination, and feedback.

Function

The people and organizations performing intelligence activities.

Information hierarchy

TermDefinition

Intelligence informationCollected information relevant to an intelligence requirement, but not necessarily fully evaluated or analyzed.

The cycle is a management model. Real practice is iterative, with continuous exchange among customers, collectors, and analysts.

What good intelligence communicates

  • What is known.
  • What is reported but unconfirmed.
  • What is assumed.
  • What is analytically judged.
  • How likely the judgment is.
  • How strong the evidentiary basis is.
  • What remains unknown.
  • What would change the judgment.

05 · Report anatomy

Five layers of a finished report

A canonical strategic assessment often follows the sequence Scope Note, Key Judgments, Discussion, and Annexes. A full product can be understood as five functional layers.

Administration

Classification, controls, report ID, title, product type, producer, publication date, information cutoff, version, audience, distribution, and contact.

Executive

Scope note, intelligence question, bottom line or executive summary, and key judgments.

Analysis

Background, current situation, discussion, outlook, scenarios, implications, indicators, warning, and intelligence gaps.

Assurance

Evidence, citations, source descriptors, source summary, assumptions, likelihood, confidence, alternatives, dissent, and methodology.

Support

Maps, charts, timelines, profiles, capability tables, glossary, probability yardstick, detailed sourcing, collection requirements, tearlines, and coordination record.

Executive sections

Scope note

Defines subject, timeframe, geography, questions, assumptions, constraints, exclusions, and information cutoff.

Intelligence question

The precise actor, issue, timeframe, and decision-relevant question the product must answer.

Executive summary

A standalone account of the principal judgment, strongest reason, expected consequence, and main uncertainty.

Key judgments

The most important analytic conclusions. They answer the question, carry appropriate uncertainty, and are supported by the body.

Main body

ScenariosDescribe distinct, plausible, decision-relevant futures.

06 · Raw reporting

Preserve source meaning and provenance

Raw reporting documents and transmits what was observed or claimed before full integration and analysis.

Typical fields

FieldPurpose
Report numberUnique retrieval, cross-reference, and evaluation identifier.
Country, area, subjectGeographic and topical scope.
Date of informationWhen the event or observation occurred.
Date of acquisitionWhen the collector obtained the information.
Date of reportWhen reporting was submitted or published.
Source descriptorProtected description of relevant access and limitations.
Source reliabilityAssessment of historical source dependability.
Information credibilityAssessment of this specific claim's plausibility and corroboration.
Summary and narrativeSalient reporting followed by detailed source content.
Collector commentsClearly separated context, clarification, or preliminary evaluation.
AttachmentsDocuments, maps, imagery, recordings, sketches, or technical data.
Controls and distributionProtection and authorized recipients.
Collection requirementThe question to which the reporting responds.

Four categories that must remain separate

  1. Direct observation: what the collector personally observed.
  2. Source statement: what another person claimed or described.
  3. Document or sensor output: what the collected artifact contains.
  4. Collector comment: interpretation or context added by the reporting officer.
Important: source reliability and information credibility are separate. A reliable source can be wrong. An untested source can be right.

Common limitations

  • Accurate but incomplete or outdated reporting.
  • Authentic information that the source misunderstood.
  • Deliberate deception or source incentives.
  • Indirect access, memory error, translation loss, or processing distortion.
  • Circular reporting that creates false corroboration.
  • Technically precise collection that lacks relevant context.
07 · Analytic assessment

Convert evidence into defensible judgment

An analytic assessment answers a defined intelligence question through integrated evidence, explicit reasoning, supported judgments, and transparent uncertainty.

Analytic chain

Question
Evidence
Evaluation
Assumptions
Alternatives
Judgment
Implications

Keep categories explicit

CategoryMeaningExample
FactSufficiently verified for the product's purpose.Imagery shows twelve vehicles at Base X.
Attributed reportingA claim preserved with source qualification.A source with direct access reports new orders.
AssumptionA supposition required to frame or bridge analysis.We assume the exercise schedule remains valid.
JudgmentA conclusion derived from evidence and reasoning.We assess the movement is probably an exercise.
ImplicationThe judgment's consequence for the customer.The movement temporarily increases escalation risk.

Likelihood is not confidence

PROBABILITY

Likelihood

How probable the proposition or event is assessed to be.

Example: An attack is likely.

BASIS

Analytic confidence

How strong and stable the evidence and reasoning supporting the judgment are.

Example: The basis for this judgment is weak.

Confidence depends on source access, evidence quality, corroboration, contradiction, methodology, assumptions, expertise, and gaps. Probability terms should be defined through a published yardstick.

Source transparency

  • Source reference citation: retrievable link to underlying reporting.
  • Appended citation: supporting reference collected outside the main text.
  • Source descriptor: protected description of access and limitations.
  • Source summary statement: holistic explanation of strengths, weaknesses, breadth, corroboration, currency, bias, and gaps.

Alternative analysis

A serious alternative states the competing explanation, supporting and contradicting evidence, why it is not the principal judgment, and the indicators that would make it more likely.

An assessment is not: a source report rewritten without attribution, a list of facts without a conclusion, a prediction presented as certainty, or an argument chosen to support preferred policy.
08 · Intelligence briefing

Tailor delivery without changing the judgment

An intelligence briefing is a concise, audience-specific presentation intended to create understanding, support a decision, or prompt appropriate action.

Standard structure

  1. Purpose: why the briefing is occurring and which decision or question it supports.
  2. Bottom line: the most important answer first.
  3. Key judgments: principal conclusions in priority order.
  4. Supporting evidence: only what is necessary to explain and defend the judgments.
  5. Uncertainty: likelihood, confidence, assumptions, gaps, and source limitations.
  6. Alternative explanation: the principal plausible competitor.
  7. Implications: why the assessment matters to this audience.
  8. Outlook and indicators: expected development and what would change it.
  9. Questions and answers: clarification and challenge.
  10. Follow-up: unanswered questions, corrections, and new requirements.

Report versus briefing

DimensionReportBriefing
FormatWritten or electronic.Usually oral and visual.
InteractionUsually asynchronous.Usually interactive.
DetailCan preserve extensive evidence.Selects and compresses.
AdaptationFixed after publication.Can adapt during delivery.
RecordDurable and citable.Ephemeral unless recorded.
StrengthPrecision and traceability.Speed, emphasis, and feedback.
RiskMay remain unread.May oversimplify caveats.

Briefing urgent raw reporting

Raw reporting may be briefed before full evaluation when urgency requires it. Status and limitations must be explicit:

This is a single-source report received 30 minutes ago. It has not been independently corroborated or fully evaluated.

Audience adaptation

Adapt selection, depth, sequence, terminology, and presentation. Never change facts, judgments, probability, or confidence to please the audience.

10 · Quality controls

What to test

Report test

  1. What question was asked?
  2. What is the answer?
  3. Why does the analyst believe it?
  4. What is fact, reporting, assumption, or judgment?
  5. How uncertain is the judgment?
  6. What could change it?
  7. Why does it matter?

Briefing test

  • Purpose explicit.
  • Bottom line first.
  • Judgments clear and prioritized.
  • Material caveats preserved.
  • Visuals necessary and sourced.
  • Questions anticipated.
  • Follow-up recorded.

Failure modes and corrections

FailureConsequenceCorrection
Source claim presented as factInflated certainty.Attribute and qualify the claim.
Collector comment merged with source statementLost provenance.Label the categories separately.
Summary substituted for analysisNo analytic value.Explain reasoning and form judgments.
Likelihood confused with confidenceMiscommunicated uncertainty.State probability and evidentiary strength separately.
Alternative mentioned but not testedToken challenge only.Compare supporting and contradicting evidence.
Briefing removes caveatsFalse certainty.Preserve material uncertainty in the bottom line.
Excessive backgroundJudgment obscured.Retain only context needed for the answer.
Recommendation drives judgmentPoliticized analysis.Separate analytic conclusions from policy preferences.

Core terminology

All-source intelligence
Intelligence integrating all relevant available collection disciplines.
Alternative analysis
Examination of a plausible explanation or outcome differing from the principal assessment.
Analytic assessment
A finished product answering a question through supported judgments and explicit uncertainty.
Analytic confidence
The strength and stability of the basis for a judgment.
Analytic judgment
A conclusion based on information, reasoning, and assumptions.
Assumption
A supposition used to frame or bridge analysis where direct information is missing.
Attribution
Identification or description of the origin of information or a claim.
Bottom line
The concise principal answer placed at the beginning of a report or briefing.
Briefing
An audience-specific presentation supporting understanding, decisions, or action.
Caveat
A qualification or limitation affecting interpretation, confidence, use, or dissemination.
Circular reporting
False corroboration caused when several reports derive from the same original source.
Collection
Acquisition of data and information to satisfy intelligence requirements.
Collector comment
Context or evaluation added by a collector and separated from source reporting.
Corroboration
Independent evidence supporting an item of information or proposition.
Data
Unprocessed observations, measurements, records, signals, images, or statements.
Dissemination
Timely delivery of intelligence to authorized customers in a suitable form.
Dissent
A documented material disagreement with a principal judgment or its reasoning.
Estimate
A forward-looking assessment of the likelihood or consequences of future developments.
Estimative language
Standard words communicating probability, such as unlikely or likely.
Evidence
Observations, reporting, data, or measurements supporting or challenging a proposition.
Finished intelligence
A product resulting from collection, processing, integration, evaluation, analysis, and interpretation.
Indicator
An observable development that supports, weakens, or changes an assessment.
Information
Data given enough context to convey meaning.
Information credibility
The assessed plausibility and corroboration of a specific information item.
Information cutoff
The latest date through which information was considered in a product.
Intelligence
Information evaluated and interpreted to reduce uncertainty and support decisions.
Intelligence gap
An important unknown limiting understanding or confidence.
Intelligence requirement
A defined knowledge need guiding collection or analysis.
Key judgment
One of a product's most important decision-relevant analytic conclusions.
Likelihood
The assessed probability that a proposition is true or an event will occur.
Methodology
The methods, definitions, models, and analytic techniques used in an assessment.
Objectivity
The obligation to base analysis on evidence and remain independent of desired outcomes.
Outlook
The expected direction of developments during a specified period.
Probability yardstick
A mapping between qualitative probability terms and approximate numerical ranges.
Provenance
The origin, chain of custody, processing history, and context of information.
Raw intelligence
Collected information not yet fully vetted, validated, integrated, or analyzed.
Raw reporting
Documentation of what was observed or claimed before full analysis.
Reliability
The assessed historical dependability and accuracy of a source.
Scenario
A coherent description of a plausible future pathway.
Scope note
The product's subject, questions, timeframe, assumptions, constraints, and exclusions.
Source descriptor
A protected description of source access, expertise, motivation, or limitations.
Source summary statement
A holistic account of the source base's strengths, weaknesses, and gaps.
Tearline
A separable or sanitized product section designed for wider authorized distribution.
Uncertainty
Remaining doubt caused by incomplete evidence, ambiguity, assumptions, or unpredictability.
Warning indicator
An observable condition associated with movement toward a defined warning scenario.
